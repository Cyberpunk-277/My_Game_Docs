# Call of Duty Campaign Rankings

*Ranked on story, pacing, mission design, and emotional impact.*

---

## S-Tier: Outstanding
*Peak storytelling, tight pacing, and set pieces people still talk about.*

**Infinite Warfare (2016)** · *Infinity Ward*
A grounded sci-fi war drama with real heart, and Ethan is some of the best character writing the series has ever done. Critics and fans who gave it a chance praised the Jackal dogfights, Kit Harington's menacing Kotch, and the way the hub-to-cockpit flow keeps the pacing tight from start to finish.

**Modern Warfare 2 (2009)** · *Infinity Ward*
Nonstop blockbuster momentum with unforgettable moments like "Whiskey Hotel" and "Cliffhanger." It was a critical darling at launch, and "No Russian" is still one of the most talked-about missions in gaming history.

**Black Ops (2010)** · *Treyarch*
Cold War paranoia at its best, with sharp twists and dialogue that still gets quoted. The Mason brainwashing storyline and the Reznov and Numbers mystery earned huge praise, and the stacked voice cast helped make it a fan-favorite for good reason.

**Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare (2007)** · *Infinity Ward*
The blueprint for the modern shooter campaign, and "All Ghillied Up" is still the benchmark for stealth missions. Reviewers at the time called it a revolution for the genre, and the nuke sequence and "Death from Above" are still held up as masterclasses in scripted pacing.

---

## A-Tier: Great
*Strong execution, each with a clear strength of its own.*

**Modern Warfare (2019)** · *Infinity Ward*
A gritty, realistic reboot that nails tension in "Clean House" and "Going Dark," and critics loved how it put moral gray areas front and center. The Highway of Death and Russia depiction drew some controversy, and a few reviewers found the back half less sharp than the opening.

**Black Ops 6 (2024)** · *Raven / Treyarch*
The most inventive campaign in years, with espionage, a casino heist, and safehouse puzzles alongside classic spectacle. Fans and critics praised the variety and the return to form, though a handful of missions felt more forgettable than the standouts.

**Black Ops Cold War (2020)** · *Raven / Treyarch*
Great 80s spycraft with branching outcomes, evidence boards, and some properly trippy psychological sequences. Reviewers praised the atmosphere and the Perseus hunt, but the runtime is on the short side and the ending left some players wanting more.

**Black Ops III (2015)** · *Treyarch*
Big, strange transhumanist ideas, with fluid movement and a co-op mode that fans still rave about. The story gets tangled in its own concepts, though, and many critics and players found it hard to follow when played solo.

---

## B-Tier: Good
*Solid play, held back by story problems or bloat.*

**Modern Warfare II (2022)** · *Infinity Ward*
Great visual fidelity, real chemistry in Task Force 141, and standout missions like the stealth boat infiltration. The survival-crafting stretches and a bloated third act drew plenty of complaints, and many fans felt the momentum fizzled out late.

**Advanced Warfare (2014)** · *Sledgehammer*
Exo-suit movement feels fresh, and Kevin Spacey's villain performance was widely praised at launch. The story is predictable and falls back on generic military-thriller tropes in the third act, which kept it from ranking higher with critics.

**Call of Duty 2 (2005)** · *Infinity Ward*
A WWII classic that introduced regenerating health and delivered memorable moments like Pointe du Hoc. It was groundbreaking for its time, but by today's standards the corridor-and-wave design feels dated and repetitive.

**World at War (2008)** · *Treyarch*
Brutal, oppressive Pacific and Eastern Front combat with a gritty tone nobody else has matched, and Gary Oldman's Reznov is a series highlight. Critics also noted the Pacific stretches get repetitive, leaning heavily on ambush and banzai charge setups.

**WWII (2017)** · *Sledgehammer*
Gorgeous visuals and a sincere return to the franchise's roots, with the Normandy opening landing especially well. Reviewers found the story and characters too familiar and played it too safe, so it rarely rises above competent.

---

## C-Tier: Disappointing
*Narrative misfires or design choices that didn't land.*

**Vanguard (2021)** · *Sledgehammer*
The Stalingrad sniper sequences with Polina were a bright spot, and the game looks great. Unfortunately, the flashback origin stories chop up the narrative, the villain is forgettable, and most critics found the whole thing disjointed.

**Black Ops II (2012)** · *Treyarch*
Raul Menendez is a standout villain, and the branching choices were ahead of their time. But the jarring era jumps, the uneven Strike Force RTS missions, and a messy overall structure left many fans and critics feeling the potential was wasted.

**Ghosts (2013)** · *Infinity Ward*
The post-apocalyptic setup was promising, and Riley the dog became an instant fan favorite. Reviewers found the characters flat, the villain bland, and the ending an unresolved cliffhanger for a sequel that never came.

**Modern Warfare 3 (2011)** · *Infinity Ward / Sledgehammer*
It wraps up the trilogy with big explosions and a few memorable set pieces like Paris. Most critics felt it was more of the same and leaned on shock value over real tension, and the story's conclusion felt rushed.

**Black Ops 7 (2025)** · *Treyarch*
Building the campaign around online co-op was a polarizing decision that critics and fans widely panned. Solo play feels hollow without AI squadmates, and the story beats turn into generic objective checklists.

---

## D-Tier: Botched
*Failures of campaign design and pacing.*

**Modern Warfare III (2023)** · *Sledgehammer*
Widely regarded as one of the weakest campaigns in the series, with a rushed 3 to 4 hour runtime and little story to speak of. The "Open Combat Missions" drop you into recycled Warzone maps to loot crates and shoot bots, and reviewers and fans alike called it lazy and unfinished.

---

### Notes
- **Black Ops 4 (2018)** is excluded because it shipped without a campaign.
- **Modern Warfare 4 (2026)** is excluded pending release.
