# The Legend of Zelda – Definitive Game Rankings & Modern Playing Guide (2026)

_Last updated: November 10, 2026_

**Critics + fans + gameplay quality + replayability + modern accessibility**

This guide ranks the mainline Zelda adventures in separate 3D and 2D categories, with multiplayer spin-offs listed separately. It also explains why each game deserves its position and identifies the best practical way to play it today.

The rankings are an editorial synthesis, not an official Nintendo ranking or a literal average of review scores and fan polls.

## Ranking methodology

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Critical reception | 30% |
| Fan reception | 30% |
| Gameplay, design & innovation | 20% |
| Replayability & modern quality of life (QoL) | 10% |
| Legacy & franchise influence | 10% |

**How the factors are applied**

- **Critical reception:** Review consensus and historical critical reputation. Scores are considered in the context of each game's original platform and release period.
- **Fan reception:** Community polls, long-term player sentiment, and the reputation games have developed over time. No single poll is treated as representative of every Zelda fan.
- **Gameplay, design & innovation:** Dungeon design, exploration, puzzles, combat, world design, pacing, and how successfully each game executes its ideas.
- **Replayability & modern QoL:** How rewarding the game is to revisit, plus the convenience of modern controls, menus, save features, accessibility, and updated releases.
- **Legacy & influence:** Historical importance and the game's lasting effect on Zelda and adventure games.

**Important distinction:** The best Zelda game, the most beloved Zelda game, and the easiest version to play today are not necessarily the same thing. Modern accessibility informs the version recommendation; it does not automatically make a newer game rank higher.

# 1. 3D Zelda games

Seven major 3D adventures, ranked by combined critical reputation, fan appreciation, design quality, replayability, and franchise impact.

## #1 – Ocarina of Time (1998)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch 2 remake (2026), where available.  
**Alternative:** Nintendo 3DS remake or the original N64 version through Nintendo Switch Online.

**Why it ranks here:** The defining 3D Zelda experience. Its dungeon structure, time-travel mechanics, music, characters, and lock-on combat established a template for 3D adventure games. It remains one of the most critically acclaimed games ever released.

**Fan perspective:** Frequently considered the franchise's greatest achievement, although some fans prefer Majora's Mask or Breath of the Wild.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent replay value, memorable dungeons, and a strong speedrunning and challenge-run community. The 3DS version offers a convenient portable remake; the N64 version preserves the original experience. The 2026 Switch 2 remake is the most thoroughly modernized option if its release and features are available in the player's region.

## #2 – Breath of the Wild (2017)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch 2 Edition.

**Why it ranks here:** Completely reinvented what a Zelda game could be. Its open-world exploration, freedom, physics, environmental storytelling, and sense of discovery made exploration feel genuinely rewarding.

**Fan perspective:** Widely loved for its freedom and atmosphere. Some traditional Zelda fans miss the larger, more structured dungeons and item progression of earlier games.

**Replayability & QoL:** Outstanding replayability through exploration, experimentation, challenge runs, and alternate routes. The Switch 2 Edition improves presentation and performance and includes ZELDA NOTES features.

## #3 – Majora's Mask (2000)

**Best modern version for most players:** Nintendo 3DS remake (2015).  
**Best for original gameplay fidelity:** N64 version through Nintendo Switch Online on Switch 2, or original N64 hardware.

**Why it ranks here:** Arguably the most distinctive Zelda adventure. Its three-day cycle, mask transformations, side quests, emotional storytelling, and unsettling atmosphere give it an identity unlike any other entry.

**Fan perspective:** Particularly beloved by longtime fans. Its unusual structure and darker tone have earned it an especially passionate following.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent side quests and character interactions reward repeat visits. The 3DS remake adds convenient item management, event tracking, and more flexible time management.

**Version caveat:** The 3DS remake changes several mechanics and boss encounters. Choose it for convenience and QoL; choose N64 for the original balance, atmosphere, and boss designs.

## #4 – Twilight Princess (2006)

**Best modern version:** Wii U HD (2016).

**Why it ranks here:** Arguably the strongest traditional 3D Zelda experience after Ocarina of Time. It offers excellent dungeons, memorable bosses, a darker atmosphere, and a compelling companion character.

**Fan perspective:** A major fan favorite, particularly among players who prefer traditional Zelda progression, larger dungeons, and a more cinematic adventure.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent dungeon and story replay value. The HD version improves presentation and streamlines selected elements. There is no native Switch or Switch 2 HD port.

## #5 – The Wind Waker (2002)

**Best modern version:** Wii U HD (2013).  
**Alternative:** Original GameCube version through Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack on Switch 2, where available.

**Why it ranks here:** One of gaming's most timeless art styles. Its ocean exploration, expressive characters, music, and sense of adventure have aged exceptionally well.

**Fan perspective:** Its art style was initially controversial but has become one of the most celebrated visual directions in the franchise.

**Replayability & QoL:** Very enjoyable to revisit. The Wii U HD version improves visuals and sailing convenience. The Switch 2 NSO GameCube release is the original GameCube game, not the HD remaster.

## #6 – Tears of the Kingdom (2023)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch 2 Edition.

**Why it ranks here:** An extraordinary technological and creative achievement. Its building system, underground exploration, sky islands, and freedom of experimentation create an enormous gameplay sandbox.

**Fan perspective:** Praised for creativity and sheer amount of content. Some players prefer Breath of the Wild's more focused exploration and stronger sense of first-time discovery.

**Replayability & QoL:** Arguably the most replayable Zelda in terms of experimentation and player-created solutions. The Switch 2 Edition improves presentation and performance and includes ZELDA NOTES features.

## #7 – Skyward Sword (2011)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch HD (2021).

**Why it ranks here:** Excellent story, memorable characters, creative dungeons, and an important place in Zelda lore. It explores the origins of the Master Sword and the recurring conflict at the heart of the franchise.

**Fan perspective:** Appreciated for its story and dungeon design, but more divisive because of the original Wii motion controls and linear structure.

**Replayability & QoL:** The Switch HD version adds button-based controls, making the game substantially more approachable for players who disliked the Wii motion controls.

# 2. 2D Zelda games

Ten top-down and handheld adventures, ranked by traditional Zelda quality, critical reception, fan appreciation, replayability, and lasting influence.

## #1 – A Link to the Past (1991)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch Online SNES.

**Why it ranks here:** The definitive traditional top-down Zelda formula. Its interconnected world, Light and Dark Worlds, dungeons, items, and progression became the blueprint for decades of Zelda games.

**Fan perspective:** An essential classic that remains one of the most respected 2D adventures.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent replay value, a strong progression system, and relatively timeless controls. The original SNES version remains a fantastic experience.

## #2 – A Link Between Worlds (2013)

**Best modern version:** Original Nintendo 3DS release.

**Why it ranks here:** An exceptionally polished adventure that modernizes A Link to the Past without losing its identity. The wall-merging mechanic, flexible dungeon progression, and excellent pacing make it one of the smartest Zelda designs.

**Fan perspective:** Frequently praised as one of the most enjoyable and underrated modern Zelda adventures.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent pacing, minimal unnecessary backtracking, and flexible dungeon order make it especially easy to revisit.

## #3 – Link's Awakening (1993)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch remake (2019).

**Why it ranks here:** A charming adventure with surprisingly emotional storytelling, imaginative dungeons, and a memorable conclusion. Its unusual setting gives it a personality unlike most other Zelda games.

**Fan perspective:** A beloved classic, with the remake introducing the adventure to a new generation.

**Replayability & QoL:** The remake improves presentation, controls, and inventory management. Its shorter length also makes it an excellent game to replay.

## #4 – The Minish Cap (2004)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch Online GBA.

**Why it ranks here:** Beautiful pixel art, excellent music, clever shrinking mechanics, and satisfying exploration. Its unique world interactions and compact design make it a standout.

**Fan perspective:** Frequently considered one of the most underrated Zelda games. Its reputation among dedicated fans is particularly strong.

**Replayability & QoL:** A relatively short, focused adventure with little wasted time. Its straightforward controls and charming presentation have aged very well.

## #5 – Oracle of Ages / Oracle of Seasons (2001)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch Online Game Boy.

**Why they rank here:** Two complementary adventures with excellent puzzles, memorable mechanics, and an interconnected linked-game system. Ages emphasizes puzzle-solving and time travel, while Seasons emphasizes action and environmental changes.

**Fan perspective:** A highly respected pair that some fans consider among the best handheld Zelda experiences.

**Replayability & QoL:** Particularly high because playing both games and linking their stories provides additional content. Best experienced as a pair.

## #6 – Echoes of Wisdom (2024)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch.

**Why it ranks here:** A significant franchise milestone with Zelda as the playable protagonist. Its Echo system creates creative puzzle-solving and combat opportunities.

**Fan perspective:** Praised for originality and creativity, although some players prefer the traditional sword-and-item combat of older games.

**Replayability & QoL:** Lots of room for experimentation and multiple ways to solve puzzles. Its modern interface is convenient, although performance can be less consistent than the best-optimized Switch games.

## #7 – The Legend of Zelda (1986)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch Online NES.

**Why it ranks here:** The game that started everything. Its non-linear exploration, secrets, and sense of discovery were revolutionary.

**Fan perspective:** Historically essential, but modern players can find its lack of direction frustrating.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent for challenge runs and exploration, but limited guidance and dated mechanics make it harder to recommend as a first Zelda.

## #8 – Phantom Hourglass (2007)

**Best modern version:** Original Nintendo DS release.

**Why it ranks here:** An enjoyable Wind Waker continuation with charming visuals, exploration, and inventive touchscreen mechanics.

**Fan perspective:** Generally regarded as a fun but less memorable handheld Zelda. Repeated visits to the Temple of the Ocean King are divisive.

**Replayability & QoL:** A relatively compact adventure, but its touchscreen-dependent design makes it inconvenient to experience on modern hardware. There is no native Switch or Switch 2 port and no official modern remake.

## #9 – Spirit Tracks (2009)

**Best modern version:** Original Nintendo DS release.

**Why it ranks here:** A surprisingly charming adventure with excellent music, a memorable Zelda companion, and strong dungeon design.

**Fan perspective:** Often considered better than its reputation suggests, especially for its story and character interactions.

**Replayability & QoL:** Good dungeon and story replay value, but train-based overworld travel, touchscreen controls, and microphone features make it difficult to play conveniently today. There is no native Switch or Switch 2 port and no official modern remake.

## #10 – Zelda II: The Adventure of Link (1987)

**Best modern version:** Nintendo Switch Online NES.

**Why it ranks here:** A fascinating experiment with RPG progression, side-scrolling combat, and challenging dungeons.

**Fan perspective:** One of the most divisive mainline entries. It has a dedicated following, but its departure from the traditional Zelda formula is not for everyone.

**Replayability & QoL:** Interesting for challenge runs and historical exploration, but its punishing difficulty and dated mechanics make it the least approachable game on this list.

# 3. Multiplayer Zelda trilogy

These games deserve recognition, but their multiplayer-first design makes them difficult to rank against traditional single-player Zelda adventures.

## #1 – Four Swords Adventures (2004)

**Original platform:** GameCube.  
**Best way to play:** Original GameCube hardware and compatible multiplayer equipment.

**Why it stands out:** A substantial level-based adventure with cooperative puzzles, clever use of multiple Links, and an enjoyable progression system. The strongest of the three as a full multiplayer experience.

## #2 – Four Swords (2002)

**Original platform:** Game Boy Advance; bundled with A Link to the Past.  
**Best way to play:** Original GBA version with compatible multiplayer hardware. The Anniversary Edition was a limited-distribution release, not a standard currently available NSO GBA title.

**Why it stands out:** A foundational multiplayer experiment that makes cooperation and competition part of the Zelda experience.

## #3 – Tri Force Heroes (2015)

**Original platform:** Nintendo 3DS.  
**Best way to play:** Original 3DS hardware.

**Why it stands out:** Excellent cooperative puzzle ideas, creative item combinations, and a unique three-player structure. Designed around three-player cooperation, although solo play is supported.

## Important version recommendations

### Ocarina of Time – Switch 2 vs. 3DS vs. N64

The Switch 2 remake is the most thoroughly modernized option if available. The 3DS remake remains a convenient portable version, while the N64 release preserves the original presentation and gameplay.

### Majora's Mask – 3DS vs. N64

**Default recommendation: 3DS.** It is more convenient thanks to improved item management, event tracking, and time controls.

**Purist recommendation: N64.** It preserves the original gameplay balance, boss encounters, and mechanics. The 3DS version changes some boss encounters and other gameplay details.

### The Wind Waker – Wii U HD vs. GameCube

**Best overall: Wii U HD.** It improves visuals and sailing convenience.

The Switch 2 NSO GameCube version is the original GameCube game, not the HD remaster.

### Twilight Princess – Wii U HD

The best official remastered version. There is no native Switch or Switch 2 HD port.

### Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom – Switch 2 Editions

Both editions offer improved presentation and performance, with additional Switch 2 features. They are enhanced editions rather than complete remakes.

## The DS accessibility problem

**Phantom Hourglass and Spirit Tracks remain the most inconvenient mainline Zelda adventures to play on modern hardware.**

- Neither has a native Nintendo Switch or Switch 2 version.
- Neither has an official modern remake.
- Both depend heavily on touchscreen controls.
- Spirit Tracks also uses microphone functionality.
- Compatible Nintendo DS and 3DS-family hardware remains the straightforward official option.

**Collection recommendation:** Do not overlook these two if building a complete Zelda collection. They are the biggest gaps in modern platform accessibility.

## Final verdict

This guide keeps 2D and 3D Zelda separate because they offer different kinds of gameplay and should not be forced into one ranking.

The most important distinction is that **the best Zelda game, the most beloved Zelda game, and the best version to play today are not necessarily the same thing.**

- **Ocarina of Time** has the strongest overall legacy.
- **Majora's Mask** has an especially passionate fan following.
- **Breath of the Wild** represents the greatest reinvention.
- **A Link to the Past** remains the classic 2D benchmark.
- **A Link Between Worlds** is one of the best modern interpretations of traditional Zelda.
- **Tears of the Kingdom** is arguably the franchise's greatest gameplay sandbox.

Replayability and modern QoL are included without unfairly penalizing older games simply because they have not received modern remakes.

**This is the master reference for ranking, collecting, and playing through the Zelda franchise.**

# 8. Zelda game sales – original releases, remakes, remasters & re-releases

_Last sales-data review: November 17, 2026_

This section tracks reported worldwide sales for the games in this guide, including separately released remakes, remasters, and enhanced editions. The goal is to show both **how each original game performed** and **how much demand its later versions generated**.

## How to read these numbers

- **Millions (M)** means millions of copies/units.
- A figure beside a platform release is that release's reported total, not necessarily the lifetime total of the underlying game across every edition.
- Nintendo's official investor-relations figures are used where available. For older titles and releases outside Nintendo's current top-selling software tables, the guide uses the latest accessible industry or community-compiled sales records. Those estimates should not be mistaken for audited Nintendo disclosures.
- **Shipments and sell-through are not identical.** Some historical sources report shipments to retailers; others report consumer sales. The source and date matter.
- Nintendo does not publish a complete, continuously updated sales ledger for every Zelda title. Sales from Virtual Console, eShop re-releases, Nintendo Switch Online libraries, and other digital re-releases are generally not broken out by title. They are therefore **not added again** to the original game's figure.
- A bundle is counted as a bundle. The Game Boy Advance release of **A Link to the Past & Four Swords** does not provide a separate sales figure for Four Swords alone.
- For **Oracle of Ages / Oracle of Seasons**, the available figure is combined; there is no reliable public split between the two games.

## Best-selling Zelda releases – key takeaways

1. **Breath of the Wild** is the clear sales leader. Nintendo reported 34.28M Switch units as of September 30, 2026; the Wii U version adds approximately 1.70M, for about **35.98M across those two original platform releases**.
2. **Tears of the Kingdom** had reached 22.90M Switch units as of September 30, 2026, according to Nintendo.
3. **Twilight Princess** is one of the strongest traditional-era sellers: approximately 8.93M across Wii and GameCube, before adding roughly 1.17M for the Wii U HD remaster.
4. **Ocarina of Time** has sold exceptionally well across generations: 7.60M on N64, 6.44M for Ocarina of Time 3D, and more than 5.2M for the Switch 2 full remake in its first four days alone.
5. **Link's Awakening** is a remake success story: the original Game Boy version is reported at 3.83M, Link's Awakening DX at 2.22M, and the 2019 Switch remake at approximately 6.63M.
6. **Phantom Hourglass** is the strongest-selling DS-era handheld Zelda in the available list, at approximately 4.76M; Spirit Tracks is reported at 2.96M.

**Confidence labels**

- **High:** Nintendo has directly published the figure.
- **Medium:** A widely repeated historical figure from a compiled sales record or established games-industry reporting; Nintendo does not currently publish a matching up-to-date total.
- **Low / approximate:** Sparse historical coverage or a figure that varies across secondary databases.

The sales appendix should be updated when Nintendo publishes new figures. Do not silently replace older platform-specific numbers with franchise-wide totals, and do not present estimated historical figures as official Nintendo disclosures.
