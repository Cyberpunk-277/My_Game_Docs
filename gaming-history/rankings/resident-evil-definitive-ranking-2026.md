# Resident Evil – Definitive Game Rankings & Modern Playing Guide (2026)

_Last updated: November 26, 2026_

**Critics + fans + survival-horror design + replayability + modern accessibility**

This guide ranks the major Resident Evil games in two eras: the **pre-RE Engine era (1996–2016)** and the **RE Engine era (2017–present)**. It covers the core numbered games, major remakes, and the most important story-driven spin-offs. Multiplayer-focused and experimental spin-offs are listed separately so they do not distort the main rankings.

The split is based on the technology and design transition beginning with **Resident Evil 7: biohazard (2017)**. It is not simply a split between old and new stories: the RE2, RE3, and RE4 remakes are RE Engine games and therefore belong in the second category, even though their original versions belong to the first.

The rankings are an editorial synthesis, not an official Capcom ranking or a literal average of review scores and fan polls.

## Ranking methodology

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Critical reception | 30% |
| Fan reception | 30% |
| Gameplay, horror & design | 20% |
| Replayability & modern quality of life (QoL) | 10% |
| Legacy & franchise influence | 10% |

**How the factors are applied**

- **Critical reception:** Review consensus and historical critical reputation, considered in the context of each game's original platform and release period.
- **Fan reception:** Long-term player sentiment, community discussion, and the reputation each game has developed. No single poll is treated as representative of every Resident Evil fan.
- **Gameplay, horror & design:** Survival-horror tension, level design, combat, resource management, puzzles, pacing, enemy design, and how successfully each game executes its intended style.
- **Replayability & modern QoL:** Route and difficulty variety, unlockables, mercenaries or extra modes, plus the convenience of current controls, performance, save systems, and modern releases.
- **Legacy & influence:** Historical importance and the game's lasting effect on Resident Evil and survival horror.

**Important distinction:** The best Resident Evil game, the scariest Resident Evil game, the most influential game, and the easiest version to play today are not necessarily the same thing. Action-heavy entries are not automatically penalized for being less frightening; they are judged on how well they deliver their particular approach.

# 1. Pre-RE Engine era (1996–2016)

This era contains the original survival-horror foundation, the fixed-camera classics, the over-the-shoulder reinvention, and the action-heavy period. It also includes the original versions of games later remade in RE Engine.

## #1 – Resident Evil 4 (2005)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 4 (2023) remake for a modern playthrough; original 2005 Ultimate HD Edition on PC or Wii edition for the original design.

**Why it ranks here:** The most influential turning point in the franchise. Its over-the-shoulder camera, aiming, crowd control, enemy reactions, encounter design, and pacing reshaped third-person action games. It is an exceptionally complete campaign, constantly introducing new threats and situations without losing its identity.

**Fan perspective:** One of the most consistently beloved entries. Some fans prefer the original trilogy's purer survival horror, but RE4's design influence and replay value are difficult to dispute.

**Replayability & QoL:** Outstanding. Difficulty modes, unlockable weapons, costumes, challenge runs, and Mercenaries give it enormous replay value. The 2023 remake modernizes the combat and presentation, but it is a reinterpretation rather than a replacement for the original.

## #2 – Resident Evil 2 (1998)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 2 (2019) remake for most players; original 1998 version via the original PC release or compatible classic-game options for historical fidelity.

**Why it ranks here:** The high point of the original fixed-camera formula. The Raccoon City Police Department, interconnected routes, A/B scenarios, Mr. X, resource management, and escalating outbreak create one of the strongest survival-horror campaigns ever designed.

**Fan perspective:** Frequently named the best classic Resident Evil. Its atmosphere and scenario structure remain a benchmark, even for players who prefer the remake's controls.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent scenario variety and route optimization. The 2019 remake is far easier to recommend today, but its two campaigns do not reproduce the original's A/B scenario structure in exactly the same way.

## #3 – Resident Evil (2002 remake)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil HD Remaster (2015).

**Why it ranks here:** The definitive version of the original mansion incident. It preserves the original's fixed-camera survival-horror structure while adding the Lisa Trevor storyline, defensive weapons, expanded rooms, improved visuals, and stronger atmosphere. It is one of the best examples of how to remake a classic without losing its identity.

**Fan perspective:** A major fan favorite, especially among players who want deliberate, traditional survival horror rather than action-oriented combat.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent. Chris and Jill campaigns, difficulty settings, alternate costumes, and route knowledge reward repeated runs. Tank controls and fixed camera angles are intentional parts of its design, though they may take adjustment for newcomers.

## #4 – Resident Evil 3: Nemesis (1999)

**Best modern version:** Original 1999 release for the complete classic experience. The 2020 remake is more convenient to control but is a separate, substantially restructured adaptation.

**Why it ranks here:** A relentless companion to RE2. Nemesis creates an unusually aggressive sense of pursuit, while branching choices, live selections, randomized item placement, and a compact campaign give the original strong replay value.

**Fan perspective:** Beloved for Nemesis, its pace, and its connection to Raccoon City's collapse. Some players prefer RE2's broader exploration and more deliberate horror.

**Replayability & QoL:** Strong. Branching moments, difficulty options, and randomization encourage repeat playthroughs. Its original controls and presentation are dated, but the design remains compelling.

## #5 – Resident Evil Code: Veronica X (2001)

**Best modern version:** Code: Veronica X on PS4/PS5 backward compatibility where available, or the Dreamcast original for the original release experience. Availability varies by region and platform.

**Why it ranks here:** A major bridge between the original trilogy and the more cinematic direction that followed. Its large environments, dual-protagonist structure, memorable villains, and ambitious story make it one of the most substantial classic-era entries.

**Fan perspective:** A cult favorite with a passionate following. Its story and atmosphere are appreciated, but difficulty spikes, backtracking, and the possibility of soft-locking a run through poor resource management make it divisive.

**Replayability & QoL:** Good for players who enjoy learning its routes and resource demands. It is less approachable than the best modernized classics and has not received a full remake.

## #6 – Resident Evil 5 (2009)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 5 Gold Edition on modern platforms.

**Why it ranks here:** A highly polished co-op action game with excellent weapon progression, satisfying combat, memorable set pieces, and a substantial campaign. It is the logical continuation of RE4's action direction, but it moves much further away from survival horror.

**Fan perspective:** Divisive as a horror sequel, but widely enjoyed as a co-op experience. Its partner system and campaign are much stronger with another human player than with an AI companion.

**Replayability & QoL:** Very high thanks to co-op, weapon upgrades, unlockables, Mercenaries, and extra content. Its age shows in some encounter design and inventory management, but it remains fun.

## #7 – Resident Evil Revelations 2 (2015)

**Best modern version:** Complete Season / Complete Edition on modern platforms.

**Why it ranks here:** A strong episodic survival-horror entry that balances exploration, resource pressure, character switching, and action. Its two paired campaigns give it a distinct structure, and its smaller environments often create a more intimate horror experience than the numbered action entries.

**Fan perspective:** Often considered one of the better spin-offs and an underrated entry. The episodic release structure and uneven pacing can hold it back.

**Replayability & QoL:** Good, especially with Raid Mode, multiple episodes, and campaign-specific character abilities. The Complete Edition is the sensible way to play.

## #8 – Resident Evil 0 (2002)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 0 HD Remaster (2016).

**Why it ranks here:** An interesting prequel with strong atmosphere, detailed environments, and a distinctive two-character system. Its item-sharing and partner mechanics create some clever situations, but the lack of item boxes and constant inventory management can become tedious.

**Fan perspective:** One of the more divisive classic entries. Its setting and visuals are appreciated, while its story, partner AI, and inventory system receive more criticism.

**Replayability & QoL:** Moderate to good. It rewards route knowledge, but the inventory friction makes it less comfortable to replay than RE1 remake or RE2.

## #9 – Resident Evil Revelations (2012)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil Revelations (2013) HD version or the later PS4, Xbox One, and Switch releases.

**Why it ranks here:** A return toward survival-horror pacing during the action-heavy period. The Queen Zenobia ship is a strong setting, and the campaign mixes exploration, combat, and episodic storytelling.

**Fan perspective:** Generally liked as a solid side entry, though its split structure and uneven secondary episodes keep it below the strongest mainline games.

**Replayability & QoL:** Raid Mode is the major strength, offering extensive combat progression beyond the campaign. The HD versions make it easier to access today.

## #10 – Resident Evil 6 (2012)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 6 on PS4, Xbox One, or PC; the later versions include the main campaign and extra modes.

**Why it ranks here:** A technically ambitious, content-heavy action game with a deep combat system, multiple campaigns, and a huge variety of enemy encounters. Its biggest weakness is a lack of focus: four campaigns, inconsistent pacing, excessive set pieces, and tonal shifts make it feel overextended.

**Fan perspective:** One of the most divisive numbered entries. Some fans enjoy its combat depth and co-op spectacle; others see it as the point where the franchise moved too far from survival horror.

**Replayability & QoL:** Substantial content, co-op, Mercenaries, and multiple campaigns. The sheer length and inconsistent quality make it a difficult game to revisit in one sitting.

## #11 – Resident Evil (1996)

**Best modern version:** Original PlayStation release or Director's Cut, depending on preference. For most players, the 2002 remake is the better way to experience the mansion story.

**Why it ranks here:** The game that established the survival-horror formula: limited ammunition, locked-room exploration, puzzles, keys, fixed camera angles, and a hostile mansion. Its influence is far greater than its modern playability.

**Fan perspective:** Historically essential and still atmospheric, but the remake has superseded it for most players.

**Replayability & QoL:** Good for historical play and speedrunning, but the original voice acting, controls, and presentation make it harder to recommend as a first entry.

# 2. RE Engine era (2017–present)

The RE Engine era begins with Resident Evil 7: biohazard. It represents a major technical and creative reset: more detailed environments, modern lighting and materials, more immersive first-person presentation, and a renewed focus on tension and vulnerability.

The era is not exclusively first-person. The RE2, RE3, and RE4 remakes use the same engine family while returning to third-person gameplay.

## #1 – Resident Evil 2 (2019 remake)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 2 (2019) on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, or PC with the next-gen update.

**Why it ranks here:** The most complete combination of classic Resident Evil design and modern presentation. The police station is an outstanding interconnected environment, Mr. X creates sustained pressure, and the combat, puzzles, exploration, and resource management work together exceptionally well.

**Fan perspective:** Frequently regarded as the best modern Resident Evil and one of the strongest remakes ever made. Some fans miss the original's more distinct A/B scenario structure.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent. Multiple characters, difficulties, unlockables, bonus modes, and route optimization make it easy to revisit. The remake modernizes the controls without losing the original's survival-horror identity.

## #2 – Resident Evil 4 (2023 remake)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 4 (2023) Gold Edition, where available, on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, or PC.

**Why it ranks here:** A superb reinterpretation of one of the most influential action-horror games ever made. It modernizes aiming, parrying, stealth, enemy behavior, level design, and character relationships while preserving the original's recognizable structure and escalating encounters.

**Fan perspective:** One of Capcom's strongest remakes. Some players still prefer the original's faster, more arcade-like pacing and specific encounters, but the remake is a remarkable standalone game.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent. New Game+, difficulty modes, weapon challenges, Mercenaries, and the Separate Ways expansion provide substantial content. Gold Edition is the most complete package.

## #3 – Resident Evil Requiem (2026)

**Best modern version:** Current PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, or PC release.

**Why it ranks here:** A major anniversary-era entry that combines two complementary approaches: Grace Ashcroft's investigative survival horror and Leon Kennedy's more action-oriented combat. Its strongest sections build an oppressive, interconnected environment and use enemy behavior to make familiar spaces feel unpredictable.

**Fan perspective:** A strong modern series highlight, particularly for its opening horror sections, dual-protagonist structure, and replay value. Its action-heavy later sections and story resolution are less consistently successful than its best environments.

**Replayability & QoL:** Excellent. First- and third-person options, difficulty settings, unlockables, and challenge runs encourage repeat playthroughs. Capcom reported 8.5 million units sold by September 30, 2026.

## #4 – Resident Evil Village (2021)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil Village Gold Edition on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, or PC.

**Why it ranks here:** A confident blend of survival horror and action. Its varied locations, memorable antagonists, strong visual identity, and progression create an adventure that feels like a collection of distinct horror experiences.

**Fan perspective:** Popular for its atmosphere, characters, and variety. Some players prefer RE7's tighter, more focused horror, while others enjoy Village's broader action and exploration.

**Replayability & QoL:** Very high. New Game+, weapon upgrades, Mercenaries, and the Shadows of Rose expansion add meaningful content. Gold Edition is the best package.

## #5 – Resident Evil 7: biohazard (2017)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 7 Gold Edition / Gold Bundle on current platforms, with the relevant DLC included.

**Why it ranks here:** The franchise's most important modern reset. The first-person perspective, isolated Baker estate, resource scarcity, grotesque family encounters, and intimate environmental design brought the series back to survival horror after RE6.

**Fan perspective:** Widely praised as a return to form. Its slower pace, first-person perspective, and tonal shift from earlier games remain divisive for a portion of longtime fans.

**Replayability & QoL:** Strong. Madhouse difficulty, unlockables, bonus modes, and story DLC add variety. The Gold package is the most complete way to experience the game.

## #6 – Resident Evil 3 (2020 remake)

**Best modern version:** Resident Evil 3 (2020) on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, or PC with the current update.

**Why it ranks here:** A visually impressive, fast-paced reimagining of Jill Valentine's escape from Raccoon City. Its combat feels responsive, Nemesis is visually imposing, and the campaign delivers some excellent action-horror sequences.

**Fan perspective:** The most controversial modern remake. The main criticism is not that it is a bad game, but that it cuts and compresses major locations, encounters, and branching elements from the 1999 original.

**Replayability & QoL:** Good combat and unlockable challenges, but the short campaign and missing original content reduce its overall value. It is best approached as a compact reinterpretation, not a replacement for Resident Evil 3: Nemesis.

## Important version recommendations

### Resident Evil 1 – 1996 original vs. 2002 remake

**Default recommendation: 2002 remake / HD Remaster.** It preserves the mansion's structure and survival-horror identity while adding substantial content and presentation improvements.

**Historical recommendation: 1996 original.** Play it to understand the franchise's origins and the evolution of fixed-camera survival horror.

### Resident Evil 2 – 1998 original vs. 2019 remake

**Default recommendation: 2019 remake.** It is the most accessible modern version and an outstanding survival-horror game.

**Classic recommendation: 1998 original.** Its A/B scenario structure, camera presentation, and original pacing are distinct enough that it remains worth playing even after the remake.

### Resident Evil 3 – 1999 original vs. 2020 remake

**Default recommendation for completeness: 1999 original.** The original has more locations, branching decisions, and a more expansive interpretation of Nemesis.

**Modern-action recommendation: 2020 remake.** Its controls and combat are excellent, but it is much shorter and omits or restructures major original sections. Play both if possible.

### Resident Evil 4 – 2005 original vs. 2023 remake

**Best modern experience: 2023 remake.** It modernizes combat, stealth, parrying, and character interactions.

**Best historical experience: 2005 original.** It has a different rhythm, encounter design, and arcade-like feel. It remains a separate essential game, not an obsolete version.

### Resident Evil 5 – Gold Edition

The Gold Edition is the sensible version because it includes the major additional content. Co-op is the defining feature; the game is much more enjoyable with a human partner.

### Resident Evil 7 and Village – Gold Editions

The Gold editions are the most complete packages, combining the core campaigns with important additional content. For first-time players, play RE7 before Village.

### Resident Evil Requiem

Requiem is a new RE Engine entry, not a remake. Its dual-protagonist structure and perspective options make it an accessible starting point in terms of controls, but its story and character relationships are more rewarding after playing RE2 and RE4.

# 5. Recommended franchise play order

There are several valid ways to experience Resident Evil. Release order is best for understanding the evolution of the series, while a modern-first order is better for players who struggle with fixed cameras and tank controls.

## Best chronological story order for a first full series run

1. Resident Evil (2002 remake)
2. Resident Evil 0
3. Resident Evil 2 (1998 original or 2019 remake)
4. Resident Evil 3: Nemesis (1999 original or 2020 remake)
5. Code: Veronica X
6. Resident Evil 4 (2005 original or 2023 remake)
7. Revelations
8. Resident Evil 5
9. Revelations 2
10. Resident Evil 6
11. Resident Evil 7: biohazard
12. Resident Evil Village
13. Resident Evil Requiem

**Timeline caveat:** Resident Evil 0 occurs before the first game, but it is generally better to play it after the 2002 remake. The original Resident Evil introduces the mansion, characters, and core mechanics before the prequel expands on them. RE3 begins before and overlaps with parts of RE2; the list keeps them in a practical player-friendly order rather than splitting either game into segments.

## Best modern-first order

1. Resident Evil 2 (2019)
2. Resident Evil 4 (2023)
3. Resident Evil 7
4. Resident Evil Village
5. Resident Evil Requiem
6. Resident Evil 1 (2002 remake)
7. Resident Evil 3 (1999 original)
8. Code: Veronica X
9. Resident Evil 5
10. Resident Evil 6
11. Revelations
12. Revelations 2
13. Resident Evil 0

This order starts with accessible modern controls and then moves backward into the classic design. It is not chronological, but it gives newcomers a strong sample of the franchise's major styles.

## Best short essential playthrough

If someone only wants the most important games:

- **Resident Evil 1 (2002 remake):** The mansion and original survival-horror formula.
- **Resident Evil 2 (2019):** The modern classic.
- **Resident Evil 4 (2023):** The action-horror reinvention.
- **Resident Evil 7:** The modern horror reset.
- **Resident Evil Village:** The evolution of RE7's formula.
- **Resident Evil Requiem:** The latest mainline evolution.

Add the original RE2 and RE3 if you want to understand the classic Raccoon City era in its original form.

# 8. Resident Evil game sales – combined editions, ports, remakes & rereleases

_Last sales-data review: November 26, 2026. Latest Capcom Platinum Titles snapshot used: September 30, 2026._

This section answers a different question from Capcom's standard sales chart: **how many copies have all the commercially reported versions of a particular Resident Evil game sold when its ports, rereleases, enhanced editions, and remakes are considered together?**

For example, Resident Evil 4 is not just the 17.1 million copies of the 2023 remake. Capcom separately reports sales for the 2005 GameCube original, PS2 port, Wii Edition, PC Ultimate HD Edition, later console ports, and Switch release. Adding those disclosed edition figures produces a much larger *reported release total*.

## How these combined totals work

- **Edition-level total:** The sum of the separate sales entries Capcom reports for the same game or closely related release family.
- **Not a unique-player count:** A person who bought the same game on GameCube and later on PS2 can appear in both edition sales totals. The sum measures units sold across releases, not distinct customers.
- **Remakes are included:** A remake is counted as another release in the same underlying game family for this section, even though it is a separate game for the ranking sections above.
- **Do not add bundles blindly:** Gold Editions, collections, and compilations can overlap with the base game or with other entries. Where overlap is plausible, I show the figure separately rather than pretending it is a clean additive total.
- **Only disclosed figures are counted:** Sales for versions without a separate published figure are not estimated. This particularly affects VR releases.
- **Different Capcom reporting views:** Capcom's Platinum Titles table lists separate platform/release entries for some older titles, while recent multiplatform releases are reported as one combined entry. The table below adds only the entries that can be identified in the published data.

### What about Resident Evil 4 VR?

There are two different VR products to distinguish:

- **Resident Evil 4 on Meta Quest 2 (2021):** A standalone VR adaptation of the 2005 original. Capcom's public Platinum Titles table does not provide a separate sales figure for this release, so it is **not included** in the 33.10M calculation.
- **Resident Evil 4 remake VR Mode (PS VR2):** A free VR mode for owners of the 2023 remake, not a separately sold game. Its sales are already part of the remake's 17.10M figure; do not add it again.

Therefore, **33.10M is the sum of the separately disclosed non-VR edition entries listed above**, not a fully exhaustive total for every way RE4 has ever been distributed. The actual all-format total could be higher, but the undisclosed VR sales should not be guessed.

## Final verdict

Resident Evil is best understood as a franchise that repeatedly reinvented itself without abandoning its core identity.

- **Resident Evil (2002 remake)** is the definitive expression of the original mansion survival-horror formula.
- **Resident Evil 2 (1998)** is the classic-era high point.
- **Resident Evil 4 (2005)** changed the direction of third-person action games.
- **Resident Evil 5 and 6** pushed the franchise toward large-scale action and co-op.
- **Resident Evil 7** brought the series back to vulnerability, atmosphere, and survival horror.
- **Resident Evil 2 (2019)** is the strongest overall modern package.
- **Resident Evil 4 (2023)** shows how to modernize an influential classic without losing its identity.
- **Resident Evil Requiem** demonstrates how the modern formula can combine slower horror and action-focused gameplay in one campaign.

The most important distinction is that **the original game and its remake are not interchangeable**. RE2 (1998) and RE2 (2019), RE3: Nemesis and RE3 (2020), and RE4 (2005) and RE4 (2023) are separate creative interpretations worth evaluating on their own merits.

The pre-RE Engine era established the formula and repeatedly reinvented it. The RE Engine era refined the presentation, modernized the controls, and brought the franchise back to the center of survival horror.

**This is the master reference for ranking, collecting, and playing through the Resident Evil franchise.**
